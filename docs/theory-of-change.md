# Theory of Change

## What it is

A theory of change is the explicit logic connecting what an organization
*does* to the impact it ultimately wants. It's usually laid out as a chain:

**Inputs** (what you put in — money, people, time) → **Activities** (what
you actually do) → **Outputs** (the immediate, countable result of the
activity) → **Outcomes** (the change in people's behavior, knowledge, or
circumstances that follows) → **Impact** (the long-term, higher-level
change you're actually after).

The point of writing one out isn't decoration — it forces you to state the
*assumptions* linking each step ("if X happens, then Y follows, because
Z"). It's also usually what funders and partners want to see, since it's
the difference between "we did a thing" and "we can show why the thing
should work."

## Worked example — TechTrek's school refurbishment project

| Stage | What it looks like here |
|---|---|
| Input | Old computers, volunteer time, thermal paste, Edubuntu |
| Activity | Clean and refurbish machines, install Linux, train students |
| Output | X computers refurbished and placed in Y schools |
| Outcome | Students who couldn't access a computer now use one regularly and gain digital skills |
| Impact | Those students have a real shot at participating in the digital economy — feeding back into the mission |

The assumption chain being tested: *if students get access to a working
computer and training, they'll build digital skills, because the barrier
was hardware and instruction, not interest.*

## Limitless Africs doesn't have one written down yet

The organization already has the shape of one — it just hasn't been stated
as an explicit chain:

- **Mission** (the "impact" end of the chain): improve people's quality of
  life, directly or by creating the circumstances that make a good life
  possible.
- **Areas of interest → Initiatives → Projects** (the "activities/outputs"
  end): expertise areas it expands from, the named efforts built on top of
  national priorities (Langture, HackAThorn, IdeaBank, CollectSun,
  TechTrek, BookShare), and the concrete projects under each.

What's missing is the **outcome** layer connecting the two — the explicit
"and that's why doing X leads to Y" reasoning for each initiative. Worth
writing once the initiatives above have more than one or two projects each
to generalize from.
